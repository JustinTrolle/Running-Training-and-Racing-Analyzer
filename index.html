Run Setup & Metrics to Analyze

How this works. Enter the run (or drop in a .FIT file), pick the metrics you want to track, choose how to split the run into segments, then analyze. You get raw numbers per segment, the percent change from segment to segment and from the first segment, and charts of how pace, heart rate and running mechanics drifted through the run.

Terminology used here: Cadence = full gait cycles per minute (left foot strike to left foot strike). Stride Rate = foot strikes per minute (every foot strike counts, so Stride Rate = 2 × Cadence). Most watches label Stride Rate as "cadence" in steps per minute. Stride Length here = distance covered per foot strike (what watches report), and Cycle Length = distance per full gait cycle (2 × Stride Length).

Coach's Read & Reference Ranges

Reference Ranges
MetricTypical RecreationalTrained / EliteWhat a Drift Through the Run Usually Means
Stride Rate160–175 strikes/min180–204 strikes/minFalling stride rate late in a run points to neuromuscular fatigue or loss of focus. Rising stride rate with falling stride length is the body protecting itself (shorter, quicker steps).
Cadence80–88 cycles/min90–102 cycles/minSame signal as stride rate, at half the value.
Stride Length90–110 cm (3.0–3.6 ft)120–160 cm (3.9–5.2 ft)Stride length is usually the first thing to go when the athlete fatigues. Pace lost late in a race is most often lost here, not in cadence.
Ground Contact Time250–320 ms180–230 msRising GCT means less elastic return and more time braking. Often paired with rising vertical oscillation and falling stride length.
Vertical Oscillation3.1–4.7 in (8–12 cm)1.2–3.1 in (3–8 cm)More bounce is wasted energy. Watch for VO rising as pace falls.
Vertical Ratio (VOR)8–11%5–7.5%VOR normalizes bounce to stride length. A rising VOR means more up-and-down for every foot of forward travel.
Flight Phase25–35% of the step cycle35–45% of the step cycleFlight % falls when GCT rises or stride rate falls. Less time in the air is less elastic running.
Pace:HR DecouplingUnder 5% is well-coupled aerobicallyIf HR climbs while pace falls, the efficiency factor (speed ÷ HR) drops. More than 5% decoupling across halves points to aerobic durability, pacing, heat or fueling as limiters.
Formulas. Stride Rate = 2 × Cadence. Stride Length (per strike, cm) = speed (cm/min) ÷ Stride Rate. Cycle Length = 2 × Stride Length. VOR = Vertical Oscillation ÷ Stride Length × 100. Step Cycle Time = 60,000 ms ÷ Stride Rate. Flight Time = Step Cycle Time − GCT. Flight % = Flight Time ÷ Step Cycle Time × 100. Air:Ground Ratio = Flight Time ÷ GCT. Efficiency Factor = speed (m/min) ÷ HR. Running Effectiveness = speed (m/s) ÷ (W/kg). % change = (segment − reference) ÷ reference × 100.

What-If: Cadence & Stride Length Adjustments

Speed = Stride Rate × Stride Length. Slide cadence (stride rate) and stride length up or down by up to 10% to see what it does to each segment's pace and time and to the overall finish time. The master sliders move every segment together; the per-segment sliders let you test a change in just one part of the run (for example holding stride length in the last quarter). Changes multiply: +5% cadence and +5% stride length is about +10% speed.
